## 1. Current state of the data-center product roadmap (Blackwell, Vera Rubin, Rubin Ultra, Feynman; networking; racks) — what ships now, what is announced for 2027+, delays and supply problems

### Takeaway
Vera Rubin (in the existing Oberon/NVL72-style rack) entered full production in mid-2026 and was guided to be about 20% of data-center revenue in the October 2026 quarter, with Blackwell still carrying the bulk of shipments; the 2027+ roadmap (Rubin Ultra 2H 2027, Feynman 2028) is officially "intact" according to Nvidia. The main execution blemishes are an independent (SemiAnalysis, July 2026) report that the Kyber rack for Rubin Ultra has slipped to 2028, HBM4 qualification delays early in 2026, a memory-cost squeeze that pulls gross margin down to a guided 71-72% trough, and a China data-center business that is guided at zero.

### Inferences
- Applying the reported +18% sequential growth to the roughly $14.8bn of the prior quarter implies fiscal Q2 FY2027 networking revenue of roughly $17.5bn (own calculation, not a reported figure), i.e. networking alone is now about twice AMD's entire data-center segment.
- The one-year cadence held for the chip (Rubin shipped in 2026 as promised in 2024-2025), but the system-level step-up for 2027 (Kyber/NVL576, 600kW-class racks) is the part of the roadmap with real execution risk. If SemiAnalysis is right, 2027 growth leans on more Oberon-based Rubin racks rather than a new rack generation, which is commercially manageable but weakens the "performance per rack doubles every year" narrative that rivals (AMD Helios, Google TPU racks) can attack.
- The guided gross-margin trough (71-72%) is a cost/pricing issue (memory), not a demand issue; management's planned price increases from February-April 2027 are the swing factor.
- Rubin CPX being dropped and replaced by Groq-derived LPX shows Nvidia adjusting its inference line-up on the fly; it buys rather than builds part of the low-latency inference roadmap.

Demand indicators are still rising: the big four hyperscalers plus Oracle reported about $200bn of capex in calendar Q3 2026 alone, held or narrowed 2026 guidance at roughly $135-220bn each, and three of them said 2027 will be "significantly"/"meaningfully" higher, though none gave a 2027 dollar figure and Microsoft trimmed calendar-2026 spend because of third-party capacity delays. ## 5. Strength of the software and ecosystem moat (CUDA, NIM, AI Enterprise, Omniverse, DGX Cloud) and the effect of the shift from training to inference

### Takeaway
The evidence found suggests the inference shift has so far strengthened rather than weakened Nvidia in absolute terms: one widely cited report puts its share of inference chips at about 74% in 2026 (up from 66%), and Nvidia bought in low-latency inference technology (Groq licence, about $20bn reported) and built it into the Rubin platform. ### Inferences
- If the resignation is confirmed, near-term operations (2026-2027 shipments) are largely locked in by decisions already taken: Rubin is in production, supply is committed ($279bn), and customer capacity plans and prepaid/equity-linked commitments are contractual. The exposure is further out: the Rubin Ultra/Kyber recovery, Feynman (2028), pricing discipline during the margin trough, and the personal relationships with TSMC, the memory makers and hyperscaler CEOs on which allocation and co-design have rested.
- A CFO as interim CEO signals continuity in financial execution and guidance but leaves product/technical leadership to the engineering bench (the article's mention of Ian Buck fits his public role on the data-center roadmap); an external successor from AMD, Arm or Apple would each carry different strategic signals, but that is speculation until a source confirms the search.
- The 17 November 2026 results call is the first scheduled occasion at which the company would have to address leadership, roadmap ownership and whether the supply-constrained ~70% fiscal-2028 outlook stands.
- A leadership vacuum would matter most at the margin where customers are already hedging (inference inside hyperscalers, the 2027-2028 rack generation); any acceleration of AMD/TPU/ASIC commitments after mid-November would be the signal to watch, but must be separated from the diversification trend that predates the news.
- The article's "two-thirds of AI computing power" is plausible as an installed-base figure slightly below Nvidia's revenue share (since hyperscaler ASICs are a larger share of deployed units than of merchant revenue), but it should be attributed to The Economist, not presented as an independently verified statistic.
