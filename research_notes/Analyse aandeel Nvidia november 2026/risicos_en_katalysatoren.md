## 1. Export controls, China and other regions

### Takeaway
Nvidia's China data-center business is effectively near zero and excluded from guidance: H200 sales are legal under US licences (with a 25% charge) but are choked mainly by Beijing, Blackwell remains barred, and Q3 FY2027 guidance (USD 108bn) assumes no China data-center compute revenue. Since late October 2026 the export-control debate has been re-politicised by smuggling cases and press reports of a Chinese intelligence effort aimed at CEO Jensen Huang, just as Democrats won both chambers of Congress (3 November 2026).

### Inferences
- Because guidance already assumes zero China data-center compute, China is now mostly upside optionality on the P&L, but a downside risk through politics: smuggling cases plus the Megaspeed affair give both parties reasons to tighten rules (chip tracking, licensing of Southeast Asian intermediaries), which could hit the "rest of Asia" revenue that critics allege is partly China-bound.
- The binding constraint on legal China sales in 2026 has been Beijing (customs, domestic-chip quotas, Hong Kong routing), not Washington; a US relaxation alone would not restore the pre-2025 business.
- The change of control in Congress (January 2027) and the 18 November briefing request raise the probability of legislated controls that the White House has so far resisted.

## 2. Tariffs, trade policy, Taiwan and US manufacturing

### Takeaway
The only chip tariff in force that touches Nvidia directly is the narrow 25% Section 232 duty of January 2026 (which doubles as the China "revenue share"); broad semiconductor tariffs remain a threatened second phase with investment-linked exemptions. Structural dependence on TSMC and on Taiwan-based CoWoS packaging is unchanged despite Arizona wafer starts.

### Cited Findings
- 14–15 January 2026 proclamation: Section 232 action on semiconductors; 25% tariff on certain advanced computing chips (H200, MI325X), with exclusions for chips imported for US data centers, startups and consumer uses; a possible second phase of broader tariffs plus a tariff-offset programme for domestic manufacturing; Commerce to update on the data-center chip market by 1 July 2026 — [White House fact sheet](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/01/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-takes-action-on-certain-advanced-computing-chips-to-protect-americas-economic-and-national-security/); [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/article/presidential-action-semiconductors-and-critical-minerals-under-section-232); [AJOT/Reuters](https://www.ajot.com/news/trump-imposes-25-tariff-on-imports-of-some-ai-chips)
- Framework deals with Taiwan, South Korea and Japan give preferential Section 232 treatment in exchange for US investment (duty-free import ratios tied to investment milestones); TSMC's US commitment is cited as USD 165bn in one source and USD 265bn in another; Politico is cited estimating only about 30% of TSMC's most advanced capacity would be in Arizona even at completion. Tariffs on Chinese semiconductors were postponed to June 2027 — [ROIC.ai](https://www.roic.ai/news/trump-imposes-new-tariffs-on-advanced-semiconductors-citing-national-security-01-30-2026); [Geopolitical Monitor](https://www.geopoliticalmonitor.com/?p=56574); [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/article/presidential-action-semiconductors-and-critical-minerals-under-section-232)
- 20 February 2026: the Supreme Court ruled 6–3 (Learning Resources) that IEEPA does not authorise tariffs, leaving Section 232 as the main route for broad tariffs — [ROIC.ai](https://www.roic.ai/news/trump-imposes-new-tariffs-on-advanced-semiconductors-citing-national-security-01-30-2026)
- (older) October 2025: first Blackwell wafer produced at TSMC Fab 21 in Phoenix; all CoWoS packaging is still in Taiwan, so Arizona wafers return to Taiwan; Amkor's Arizona packaging plant is expected to start production in early 2028 — [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/109958-tsmc-produces-first-nvidia-blackwell-gpu-us-but.html); [I-Connect007](https://iconnect007.com/article/147447/nvidias-blackwell-chips-made-in-arizona-still-head-to-taiwan-for-final-assembly/147444/pcb)
- Nvidia's stated plan is USD 500bn of US AI infrastructure over four years with TSMC, Foxconn, Wistron, Amkor and SPIL, including supercomputer plants in Houston (Foxconn) and Dallas (Wistron) — [Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/nvidia-american-made-ai-supercomputers-blackwell-production-begins-in-arizona/chfCarBRbaB)

### Inferences
- The data-center exemption means US tariffs currently do little to Nvidia's US demand; the risk is a phase-two tariff without adequate offsets, or loss of exemptions.
- Until US advanced packaging exists (2028 at the earliest), a Taiwan Strait disruption would halt essentially all leading-edge Nvidia supply regardless of Arizona wafer output.

### Gaps
- No confirmed status of phase-two (broad) semiconductor tariffs after the 1 July 2026 Commerce update.
- No current, sourced assessment of Taiwan Strait risk for autumn 2026 was found; the announcement date of the USD 500bn plan is given inconsistently in secondary sources (2025 versus March 2026).
- Claims of "yield parity" and high-volume Blackwell output in Arizona come from low-quality sites and are unverified.

## 3. The "AI bubble" and financing debate; bears and bulls

### Takeaway
The debate in autumn 2026 is no longer about demand today (hyperscaler capex roughly doubled year on year in Q3 2026) but about who ultimately pays: Nvidia's own financing role has grown to reported deal talks above USD 750bn, OpenAI forecasts about USD 278bn of cash burn to 2030, three of four hyperscalers have negative free cash flow, and politics (data-center backlash after the midterms) and power are new brakes. Bears focus on circularity, depreciation and China exposure; bulls on capacity shortage, earnings growth and a forward multiple that has compressed sharply.
